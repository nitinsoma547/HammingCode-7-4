# Desi diaspora voice

Use this when writing for any client of The Business. The audience is
first- and second-generation Indian-Americans in Northern Virginia. They
recognize forced "ethnic" marketing voice in two words. The whole brand
fails or succeeds on this.

## Do use

- **Specific names, real units, real hours.** "Mysore masala dosa,
  $11.99, Tue–Sun 11–9" beats "delicious dosa, all week."
- **Single regional anchor.** "Kerala-style" or "Hyderabadi" or "Punjabi"
  is information. "Authentic Indian" is filler.
- **The owner's voice when they offered it.** Phrases like "my amma's
  recipe," "we never use frozen," "kept on the menu 20 years" carry
  real signal. Use them when the brand_profile supplies them.
- **Casual numbers.** "Two seatings, 5pm and 8pm" reads better than
  "We offer evening service at multiple times."
- **English contractions.** "It's," "we're," "you'll" — formal Indian
  English reads stiff to diaspora ears.
- **Direct CTAs.** "Call to reserve" / "Tap to call" / "Walk in any
  evening" — never "We invite you to join us."

## Do NOT use

These phrases are on the universal do_not_say list — every brand
inherits them, plus their own additions:

- "delicious authentic cuisine"
- "best in town"
- "you'll love it"
- "come check us out"
- "experience the flavor"
- "a culinary journey"
- "we use only the finest"
- "our chef has years of experience"
- "made with love"
- "an explosion of flavor"
- "feast for the senses"
- "true taste of India"

## Code-switching rules

- **English-only unless brand_profile.languages says otherwise.** Don't
  sprinkle Hindi/Tamil/Malayalam words into English copy as "flavor."
  That reads as fake-ethnic.
- **OK to use specific dish/festival names untranslated.** "Pongal,"
  "sadya," "thandai," "biryani" — these are names of things, not flavor
  words. No need to italicize them either.
- **Devanagari/Tamil/Malayalam script only as a parallel headline, not
  inline.** A Devanagari sub-label above an English headline (small,
  60-80% opacity) signals "we know who you are." Devanagari mid-sentence
  signals "the marketing person Googled it."
- **Honorifics ("ji," "anna," "akka") only when speaking to a specific
  named individual** the brand actually addresses that way. Generic
  "aunties and uncles" is patronizing.

## Voice by client category

- **Restaurants:** specific dish + sensory detail + practical info. "16-
  hour fermented batter. Crisp edge. Tue–Sun 11am–9pm."
- **Spice / grocery:** specific brand or origin + cooking application.
  "Idukki cardamom — green, whole pods. Best in payasam." Not "premium
  spices for your kitchen."
- **Salons / threading / mehendi:** anchor on event, not service.
  "Pre-Karva-Chauth slots open" beats "professional eyebrow threading."
- **Tutoring centers:** anchor on outcome, not curriculum. "Algebra
  II final this Friday — we have one open slot Wed evening" beats
  "expert math instruction K-12."

## Length norms

- Social caption FB: 1-3 short sentences.
- Social caption IG: 4-8 short lines, line-broken for scannability.
- WhatsApp broadcast: 4-7 lines, no markdown, plain text.
- Email body: 80-250 words.
- Review response: 2-5 sentences.
- Flyer headline: ≤ 7 words. Subhead ≤ 12 words.

## When in doubt

Ask: would the owner's mom recognize this as how the family actually
talks about the food / shop? If no, rewrite. If you can't get a yes,
the copy isn't there yet.
