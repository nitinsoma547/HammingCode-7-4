# Product Marketing Context

*Last updated: 2026-05-16*
*Drafted in collaboration with the marketing team agents. Many sections are V1 — see "Open Gaps" at the bottom for what needs verification.*

## Product Overview

**One-liner:** Aditi Spice Depot is a small Indian grocery store in Herndon, VA — with **Aditi Kitchen**, a South Indian vegetarian eatery, inside it.

**What it does:**
- **Aditi Spice Depot** sells Indian groceries, spices, fresh vegetables, frozen and ready-to-eat items, and South Asian household products at affordable prices.
- **Aditi Kitchen** is a small in-store South Indian vegetarian restaurant serving dishes like dosa, idli, uttapam, biryani, chole bhature, and Indo-Chinese. Dine-in, pickup, and delivery on DoorDash / UberEats / Caviar / Grubhub / Seamless.

**Product categories (how customers search):**
- Indian grocery store / Indian spice store
- South Indian vegetarian restaurant / dosa restaurant / Indian vegetarian restaurant
- (NOT: Indian buffet, North Indian restaurant, Indian fine dining)

**Business type:** Local brick-and-mortar — small independent retail + counter-service restaurant under one roof.

**Pricing:** Affordable / value tier. Masala Dosa $9.99. Aditi Feast (full thali) $13.99. Chole Bhature $10.99. Grocery prices reported as competitive.

**Locations:** 600 Carlisle Dr, Herndon, VA 20170. Phone (depot): (703) 464-1100. Phone (kitchen): (703) 464-1775.

## Target Audience

The business is essentially serving **two overlapping audiences**, and only one currently knows the kitchen exists.

**Audience A — Current core (well-served):**
- Indian / South Asian families in the Herndon / Reston / Chantilly / Sterling corridor
- Coming in primarily for **groceries** — staples, spices, fresh vegetables, hard-to-find items
- Brand-loyal; many are repeat customers (this is where the 500+ reviews came from)
- The kitchen is largely invisible to them as a destination — at most an occasional add-on

**Audience B — Latent / under-served (the growth):**
- Non-Indian neighbors and local office workers in Herndon who want **authentic, affordable, vegetarian Indian food** but don't think to walk into a grocery store
- Already-Indian diners across NoVA looking for **South Indian** specifically (rarer than North Indian in the suburbs)
- Office lunchers in the Herndon business corridor (Worldgate, Carlisle, Centreville Rd) — $10 dosa is corporate-lunch material
- Delivery customers via DoorDash/UberEats who would never see the storefront

**Primary use cases:**
- "I need [specific spice / Indian staple] for tonight's cooking" → grocery
- "I want a real dosa / South Indian thali for lunch" → kitchen dine-in or pickup
- "I want Indian vegetarian delivered to my office or home" → kitchen via DoorDash/UberEats
- "I want a casual, cheap, vegetarian-friendly lunch spot" → kitchen (currently nobody discovers this way)

**Jobs to be done:**
- *Grocery:* "Get authentic Indian groceries faster/closer/cheaper than driving to Patel Brothers or Lotte"
- *Kitchen:* "Eat the South Indian food my family/I grew up with — fresh, affordable, no fuss"
- *Kitchen:* "Try a vegetarian Indian lunch without committing to a sit-down restaurant"

## Personas

This is local B2C — no formal B2B personas. The two audiences above function as personas.

## Problems & Pain Points

**The business's own pain point (from the marketer):**
- Foot traffic is not growing. The kitchen specifically is underutilized — barely any takeout or dine-in observed on visits despite being open and on delivery apps.
- The brand has equity (500+ Google reviews on the depot) but it's not translating into kitchen volume.

**Customer pain points the business solves:**
- *South Asian customer:* "Big-chain grocery doesn't carry the spices/veg/snacks I need; the nearest big Indian grocery is a longer drive."
- *Non-Indian local:* "I want to try real Indian food but the local Indian restaurants are expensive sit-downs or generic buffets. I don't want a $25 entrée."
- *Office worker:* "Lunch options in Herndon are all the same fast-casual chains. I want something cheap, fresh, vegetarian, and different."

**Why current alternatives fall short:**
- *Patel Brothers / large Indian groceries* — farther drive, less personal, no fresh kitchen on site
- *Sit-down Indian restaurants* — more expensive, more North Indian focused, slower
- *Fast-casual chains (Cava, Chipotle, etc.)* — not Indian; less variety for vegetarians who actually want Indian food
- *Other small Indian groceries in Herndon (India Bazaar, Hello 2 India, Fresh World, Madina Market, Swagat)* — most don't have a kitchen inside, or have a less developed kitchen offering

## Competitive Landscape

**Direct grocery competitors:**
- India Bazaar (Herndon area)
- Hello 2 India (Herndon area)
- Fresh World (Herndon)
- Swagat Grocery (Herndon)
- Patel Brothers (regional chain, farther but draws traffic)

→ *Falls short because:* most don't have a working in-store kitchen serving made-to-order South Indian food at $10 price points.

**Direct restaurant competitors (South Indian vegetarian in NoVA):**
- Brundavanam (Herndon) — closest direct competitor; vegetarian, South Indian leanings
- Bombay Bistro / Bombay Tandoor and similar sit-downs (more North Indian, more expensive)
- Other dosa / South Indian spots scattered across Chantilly, Fairfax

→ *Falls short because:* Aditi is **lower price point, casual counter-service, and pairs with grocery for a different value proposition.**

**Indirect competitors:**
- Fast-casual chains (Cava, sweetgreen, Chipotle) for the office-lunch dollar
- Cooking at home with groceries (which Aditi also enables — interesting tension)
- DoorDash/UberEats Indian options from outside Herndon

## Differentiation

**Key differentiators (V1 — needs validation):**
1. **Two businesses, one stop.** You can pick up groceries *and* eat a fresh dosa in the same trip. No one else in Herndon offers this combo.
2. **Affordable South Indian.** Most South Indian restaurants in the area are at higher price points. $9.99 masala dosa is positioned as accessible.
3. **Vegetarian-first.** The kitchen is fully vegetarian, which is rare and a strong positive signal for a defined audience (Indian vegetarians, Jain customers, non-Indian vegetarians, allergen-aware diners).
4. **Authentic small / family-run feel.** Not a chain. Long-tenured local business with deep ties to the Herndon Indian community.
5. **Already on every delivery app.** Lower friction to expand reach without changing the storefront.

**Why customers choose Aditi (hypothesis to validate):**
- For groceries: convenience, price, friendly staff, fresh vegetables
- For the kitchen: affordable South Indian, vegetarian, quick

## Objections

| Objection | Possible response |
|-----------|-------------------|
| "It's just a grocery store, not a restaurant" | The kitchen has its own brand (Aditi Kitchen), separate menu, 4.3⭐ on DoorDash with 100+ reviews — it's a real restaurant that happens to share a wall. |
| "I want meat / chicken options" | Vegetarian-only is intentional. South Indian food is rich and varied without meat. (Anti-persona — not a fit for meat-only diners.) |
| "I've never heard of it" | Likely the biggest real objection — it's a discovery problem, not a perception problem. |
| "Looks small / hole-in-the-wall" | Lean in: that's the appeal — affordable, authentic, fresh. |

**Anti-persona (who is NOT the customer):**
- Diners specifically seeking meat-based Indian (tandoori, butter chicken, biryani-with-chicken)
- Customers looking for upscale sit-down dining
- Customers looking for fusion / "modern" Indian (gymkhana / Rasika style)

## Switching Dynamics (JTBD Four Forces)

**Push (frustration with current option):**
- "The Indian groceries I drive to are too far"
- "The Indian restaurants near work are expensive and slow"
- "I'm vegetarian and most Indian menus skew meat-heavy"

**Pull (what draws them to Aditi):**
- South Indian specifically (rare)
- Vegetarian-only
- Cheap relative to alternatives
- One trip for groceries + lunch
- Available on the delivery app they already use

**Habit (what holds them to existing option):**
- They already drive to Patel Brothers / their regular grocery on autopilot
- They already order from their go-to Indian spot on DoorDash
- They don't know Aditi Kitchen exists as a kitchen

**Anxiety (worries about switching):**
- "Will the food be good if it's inside a grocery store?"
- "Is it actually open / consistent / clean?" (small grocery store kitchens carry a stigma — must be overcome with visible quality cues)

**Words to use:**
- Authentic, South Indian, vegetarian, fresh, dosa, affordable, family, Herndon

**Words to avoid (until validated):**
- "Restaurant" alone — under-sells the unique combo
- "Cafe" — could under-sell the menu depth
- Generic Indian — strip out the specificity and you lose the differentiation

## Brand Voice

**Tone:** Warm, family-run, approachable. Not corporate. Not aspirational-luxury.

**Style:** Conversational, a little nostalgic ("the food we grew up eating"), proud of the unfussy authenticity.

**Personality:** Authentic, generous, neighborhood, vegetarian, South Indian.

## Goals

**Business goal:**
More daily foot traffic into the store **and** more kitchen orders (dine-in, pickup, and delivery).

**Primary conversion actions (in priority order — V1 hypothesis):**
1. **Lunch dine-in / pickup at the kitchen** (highest margin, builds local repeat habit)
2. **DoorDash / UberEats orders for Aditi Kitchen** (already-existing channel, undermarketed)
3. **Grocery foot traffic from new customers** (harder to grow given crowded category)
4. **Catering orders** (if the kitchen offers this — needs confirmation)

**Constraints to be aware of (operational, not marketing):**
- The marketer cannot change the menu, prices, or operations — only recommend.
- Marketing budget is TBD and depends on the owner. Plan should be designed to **prove value cheaply first, then negotiate budget.**
- Instagram account exists but is dormant (no posts in a while). Likely can get access.
- Marketer is new to marketing — execution help and templates matter more than abstract strategy.

## Open Gaps (verify with owner / on next store visit)

1. **Year founded / owner story** — needed for an authentic narrative angle. Reviews mention "15 years" — confirm.
2. **Catering offering** — does the kitchen do catering? Big opportunity for South Asian weddings, office events, religious functions in NoVA.
3. **Hours** — confirm exact hours of depot and kitchen separately.
4. **Aditi Indian Dining in Kingstowne (myaditi.com)** — same owner / family? Same brand? Could be sister location or unrelated.
5. **"Amma's Vegetarian Restaurant" name** — historical name? Still used informally? Branding inconsistency to fix.
6. **Verbatim customer reviews** — pull 20-30 quotes directly from the Google Business Profile once access is granted, for ad copy and social.
7. **Owner's growth priorities** — is this lunch crowd? Catering? Delivery? Foot traffic specifically? Get the owner to rank.
8. **Operational capacity** — can the kitchen handle 2× current order volume without quality issues? (Critical before marketing pushes demand.)
9. **Marketing budget commitment** — what is the owner willing to spend monthly once value is shown?
10. **Owner's interest in operational changes** — e.g., visible street signage, kitchen window menu board, IG/GBP photo refresh. These cost little and matter a lot.

## Strategic Hypothesis (for the rest of the team)

**The kitchen is the growth lever, not the depot.** The depot has scale ceiling (limited by local Indian demographic + crowded category). The kitchen has uncapped reach: affordable, vegetarian, South Indian — a niche the broader Herndon population (and broader NoVA delivery radius) will pay for once they discover it.

**Strategy in one line:** Make Aditi Kitchen findable and irresistible, then let the spice depot ride the halo.

**Recommended near-term plays (for CMO + specialists to expand):**
1. Fix Google Business Profile for the **Kitchen** as a standalone restaurant listing (cheapest, highest ROI move)
2. Reactivate Instagram with a 4-week content sprint (mostly food, mostly the kitchen)
3. Cross-promote kitchen at the depot register and on every grocery customer's path
4. Lunch-focused promo aimed at Herndon office workers via Google / Instagram local ads — but only after assets are in place
5. Catering / event capability check, and if it exists, separate landing + outreach
